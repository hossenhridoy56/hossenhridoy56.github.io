A Cluster Analysis of Bangladesh's Monthly Climate and Its Comparison with the Traditional Six-Season Calendar
Md. Matiur Rahman Molla (corresponding author), Md. Hridoy Hossen, Ayesha Siddika
Submitted to Theoretical and Applied Climatology (Under Peer Review, 2026).
Abstract: Bangladesh uses a traditional six-season calendar (Grishma, Barsha, Sharat, Hemanta, Sheet, and Basanta) that predates instrumental records. We clustered the twelve calendar months using maximum and minimum temperature, rainfall, relative humidity, wind speed, and cloud cover from 33 Bangladesh Meteorological Department stations over 1985–2014 (10,865 station-months). Exhaustive search among contiguous circular partitions identified a four-season climatology: Winter (November–February), Pre-Monsoon (March–May), Monsoon (June–September), and Post-Monsoon (October). With mid-month boundaries, the traditional six seasons group into these empirical clusters in pairs. Resampling stations and years gave mean adjusted Rand indices of 0.93 and 0.88. Autoregressive Model-Based Functional Data Clustering: Resolving Temporal Dependence in Multi-Year Climatological Profiles
Md. Hridoy Hossen (Supervised by Assoc. Prof. Md. Matiur Rahman Molla)
M.Sc. Thesis Research Monograph, Department of Statistics & Data Science, Islamic University, Bangladesh (Expected April 2027).
Develops the AR-FMC framework for clustering functional curves while accounting for serial autocorrelation across repeated annual observations. Represents curves via Karhunen–Loève expansion, incorporates stationary AR(1) dynamics into latent score mixtures via a full Prais–Winsten transformation, and derives GLS estimators for the EM algorithm. Tested on 76 years of ECMWF ERA5-Land daily precipitation (1950–2025) across 1,214 grid cells in Bangladesh (92,264 individual annual curves without temporal pooling).

A Novel Massive Variance Stability Test: Theory, Distributional Derivations, and Superior Performance Under Structural Breaks
Md. Hridoy Hossen
Methodological Working Paper (2026).
Formulates a segment-dispersion test statistic for evaluating variance stationarity in long sequences. Proves asymptotic chi-square convergence under null conditions and evaluates finite-sample size and power across 10,000 Monte Carlo runs under Gaussian, Student-t, and heteroskedastic error processes.
