I am a graduate researcher in the Department of Statistics & Data Science at Islamic University, Bangladesh. I am working toward a thesis-based M.Sc. in Statistics (expected April 2027), having earned my B.Sc. in Statistics (CGPA 3.46 / 4.00, 160 credits) at the same institution. Currently, I serve as a Graduate Research Assistant on a climate statistics grant funded by the University Grants Commission of Bangladesh (UGC).

My research focuses on functional data analysis, model-based clustering, and time series econometrics, with an emphasis on environmental and climatological applications. Methodologically, I work on likelihood-based finite mixture models that account for temporal dependence across repeated functional trajectories, asymptotic properties of variance stationarity tests under structural breaks, and reproducible pipelines for multi-decadal gridded climate panels.

A Cluster Analysis of Bangladesh's Monthly Climate and Its Comparison with the Traditional Six-Season Calendar
Md. Matiur Rahman Molla (PI), Md. Hridoy Hossen, Ayesha Siddika
Submitted to Theoretical and Applied Climatology (Under Review, 2026).
We clustered the twelve calendar months using 30 years of meteorological records (1985–2014) across 33 BMD weather stations (10,865 station-months) over six variables (Tmax, Tmin, rainfall, humidity, wind, and cloud cover). Using an exhaustive circular search among contiguous partitions and Ward linkage, we found a four-season partition (Winter, Pre-Monsoon, Monsoon, Post-Monsoon) as the most stable description of the monthly climatology. The traditional six seasons map into these data-based clusters in pairs. Supported by the University Grants Commission of Bangladesh.

Autoregressive Model-Based Functional Data Clustering: Resolving Temporal Dependence in Multi-Year Climatological Profiles
Md. Hridoy Hossen (Supervised by Assoc. Prof. Md. Matiur Rahman Molla)
M.Sc. Thesis Project, Department of Statistics & Data Science, Islamic University, Kushtia (In Progress, Expected April 2027).
  • Core Innovation: Formulating a likelihood-based finite mixture framework for repeated annual functional trajectories with an explicit Gaussian AR(1) dependence structure across observation years, relaxing classical independence assumptions without forcing static pre-averaging.
  • Mathematical Derivations: Evaluated on pooled Functional Principal Component Analysis (FPCA) score vectors $\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i,t} \in \mathbb{R}^d$. Incorporated a Prais–Winsten transformation with exact lower-triangular Jacobian $(1-\phi^2)^{d/2}$ preserving observed-data likelihood: $$\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\Theta}) = \prod_{i=1}^N \left[ (1 - \phi^2)^{d/2} \sum_{g=1}^G \pi_g \prod_{t=1}^T f_d\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{i,t};\, a_t(\phi)\boldsymbol{\mu}_g,\, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g\right) \right]$$
  • Computational Estimation: Generalized EM algorithm with numerically optimized autoregressive parameter $\phi$, GLS mean updates, BIC/ICL cluster selection, and effective sample size scaling $T_{\text{eff}} = T\frac{1-\phi}{1+\phi}$.
  • Application Dataset: High-resolution ERA5-Land daily precipitation reanalysis for Bangladesh (1950–2025; 76-year continuous panel across 1,214 grid cells, clustering 92,264 individual annual curves).

A Novel Massive Variance Stability Test: Theory, Distributional Derivations, and Superior Performance Under Structural Breaks
Md. Hridoy Hossen
Methodological Working Paper (2026).
Derives the asymptotic chi-square distribution of a segment-dispersion test statistic under the null of variance stationarity. Monte Carlo experiments (10,000 replications) evaluate empirical size and power relative to the Breusch–Pagan and Goldfeld–Quandt tests under structural variance shifts and fat-tailed innovations.
