Research Statement & Agenda

Functional Trajectories, Variance Stationarity & Climate Dynamics

Modern scientific phenomena frequently produce data that are naturally represented as continuous curves, temporal processes, or high-dimensional spatial surfaces. In these settings, standard statistical assumptions of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) replications routinely fail.

My research program develops principled statistical methods designed to respect underlying temporal dependencies, asymptotic distributional behavior, and spatial heterogeneity. My work spans theoretical derivations, high-throughput Monte Carlo simulations, and applications in multi-decadal climatology and econometrics.

1. Functional Data Analysis with Temporal Dependence

In climatological studies, an annual rainfall trajectory is represented as a functional curve $Y_{i,t}(s)$ over a season $s$. Standard functional clustering algorithms treat curve observations across repeated years as mutually independent given cluster membership. However, annual curves exhibit substantial multi-year persistence. Ignoring this serial correlation causes model selection criteria (such as BIC) to over-estimate the number of true underlying clusters.

Model Formulation:

Pooled Functional Principal Component Analysis (FPCA) maps curves into $d$-dimensional score vectors $\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i,t} \in \mathbb{R}^d$. Given cluster label $z_i = g$, the scores evolve according to a stationary vector AR(1) process:

$$\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i,t} = \boldsymbol{\mu}_g + \boldsymbol{u}_{i,t}, \quad \boldsymbol{u}_{i,t} = \phi \boldsymbol{u}_{i,t-1} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i,t}, \quad |\phi| < 1, \quad \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{i,t} \sim \mathcal{N}_d(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g)$$

Applying a Prais–Winsten transformation yields independent innovations while preserving the lower-triangular Jacobian $(1-\phi^2)^{d/2}$ directly inside the observed-data likelihood:

$$\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\Theta}) = \prod_{i=1}^N \left[ (1 - \phi^2)^{d/2} \sum_{g=1}^G \pi_g \prod_{t=1}^T f_d\left(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{i,t};\, a_t(\phi)\boldsymbol{\mu}_g,\, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g\right) \right]$$

Key Project:
Model-Based Clustering of Climatological Functional Data with Temporal Dependence: An Autoregressive Mixture Model for Bangladesh Monsoon Rainfall (M.Sc. Thesis Project, Islamic University, Kushtia; Evaluated on 76 years of ERA5-Land daily precipitation).

2. Time Series Econometrics & Asymptotic Distribution Theory

Checking whether variance remains stable across time is essential in financial econometrics and macroeconomic forecasting. Traditional homoskedasticity tests often require prior knowledge of structural break dates or struggle under heavy-tailed non-Gaussian innovations.

Massive Variance Stability Test (MVT):

A time series of length $n$ is partitioned into $k$ non-overlapping segments of size $m = n/k$. The MVT statistic measures dispersion among segment-wise variances:

$$Q_{\mathrm{MVT}} = \frac{m-1}{2\bar{v}^2} \sum_{j=1}^k (v_j - \bar{v})^2 \overset{d}{\longrightarrow} \chi^2_{k-1}$$

Analytical noncentrality parameters were derived for single and multiple structural variance shifts occurring at segment boundaries.

Key Project:
A Novel Massive Variance Stability Test: Theory, Distributional Derivations, and Superior Performance Under Structural Breaks. Validated across 10,000 Monte Carlo replications and 4,997 daily Gold Futures price observations ($p \approx 7.67 \times 10^{-258}$).

3. Climatological Reclassification & Machine Learning (UGC Funded)

Climate change is altering traditional seasonal timing across South Asia. We conducted a comprehensive empirical evaluation challenging the centuries-old traditional six-season calendar paradigm of Bangladesh.

UGC Funded (FY 2025–2026) Under Peer Review Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23324622

Key Project:
Molla, M. M. R., Hossen, M. H., & Siddika, A. (2026). A Cluster Analysis of Bangladesh's Monthly Climate and Its Comparison with the Traditional Six-Season Calendar. Submitted to Theoretical and Applied Climatology.
• Analyzed 30 years (1985–2014; 10,865 station-months) across 33 BMD stations over 6 meteorological variables.
• Demonstrated strong empirical support for a 4-season structure (Winter, Pre-Monsoon, Monsoon, Post-Monsoon) with October as an independent transition month.

4. Spatial Environmental Statistics & Public Health Modeling

Understanding spatial vulnerability requires synthesizing high-resolution climate reanalysis with granular demographic and epidemiological surveys.

Key Project:
Climate-Health Vulnerability Assessment in Bangladesh: A Data-Driven Statistical Framework.
Applied Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to seven multi-decadal ERA5-Land climate indicators across all 64 districts (capturing 89.73% of total variance), linking with MICS 2025 health and disaster indicators to formulate standardized composite indices for Climate Exposure, Health Sensitivity, and Adaptive Capacity.
