Research Overview

Statistical methodology, likelihood formulations, and climatological applications

Many environmental and economic systems produce data naturally represented as continuous curves, temporal sequences, or spatial fields. In these settings, standard assumptions of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) observations rarely hold.

My research focuses on developing likelihood-based statistical methods that respect temporal serial dependence, structural breaks, and spatial heterogeneity. My work combines theoretical derivations, Monte Carlo simulations, and applied studies in multi-decadal climatology.

1. Functional Data Analysis with Temporal Dependence

In climatological monitoring, continuous annual records (such as seasonal precipitation accumulation) are represented as functional trajectories over time. Standard functional clustering algorithms treat curve observations across repeated years as mutually independent realizations. However, multi-year climate panels exhibit serial autocorrelation driven by atmospheric memory and low-frequency climate modes.

A common workaround is to average multiple years of curves into a single profile. While this avoids modeling the serial dependence, it removes year-to-year variation, extreme events, and shifts in seasonal transitions.

AR-FMC Formulation:

1. Dimensionality reduction: Each curve is projected onto orthogonal eigenfunctions obtained via Functional Principal Component Analysis (FPCA):

$$X_i(t) = \mu(t) + \sum_{k=1}^K \xi_{ik}\phi_k(t) + \epsilon_i(t), \quad \boldsymbol{\xi}_i = (\xi_{i1}, \dots, \xi_{iK})^\top \in \mathbb{R}^K$$

2. Latent AR(1) dynamics with Prais–Winsten likelihood: Conditional on cluster membership $k$, the score vector follows a stationary first-order autoregressive process:

$$\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_k = \phi (\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i-1} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_k) + \boldsymbol{\eta}_{ik}, \quad \boldsymbol{\eta}_{ik} \sim \mathcal{N}_K(\mathbf{0}, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k)$$

To retain the initial observation under stationarity, we implement a full Prais–Winsten transformation, ensuring $\mathrm{Var}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1} \mid z_{i1}=1) = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_k / (1 - \phi^2)$.

3. EM estimation: Parameter estimation uses an Expectation–Maximization algorithm, where cluster centers $\boldsymbol{\mu}_k$ are updated via Generalized Least Squares (GLS) to account for temporal covariance.

Application to Bangladesh (1950–2025): Deployed on ECMWF ERA5-Land daily precipitation covering 1,214 grid cells across Bangladesh over 76 years. Each grid-cell year was converted into a 122-day cumulative monsoon profile (1 June to 30 September), yielding 92,264 individual annual curves clustered without pre-averaging. The five-cluster solution identified distinct regimes, such as early heavy accumulation in the northeastern haor basin versus delayed, drier profiles in the northwestern Barind tract.

2. Time Series Econometrics & Variance Stationarity (MVT)

Testing whether volatility remains stationary across long horizons is essential in financial econometrics and macroeconomic forecasting. Traditional homoskedasticity tests frequently require pre-specifying break dates or suffer size distortions under non-Gaussian, fat-tailed innovations.

Massive Variance Stability Test (MVT):

Partitioning a series of length $n$ into $k$ non-overlapping segments of size $m = n/k$, the MVT statistic tests segment sample variances $s_j^2$ against a global variance $s_0^2$:

$$Q_{\mathrm{MVT}} = \frac{m}{2} \sum_{j=1}^k \left( \frac{s_j^2 - s_0^2}{s_0^2} \right)^2 \overset{d}{\longrightarrow} \chi^2_{k-1} \quad \text{as } m \to \infty$$

Monte Carlo experiments (10,000 replications) evaluate empirical size and power relative to the Breusch–Pagan and Goldfeld–Quandt tests under structural variance shifts and fat-tailed innovations.

3. Climatological Reclassification & Cluster Analysis (UGC Project)

As a Research Assistant on a project funded by the University Grants Commission (UGC) of Bangladesh, I examined whether the traditional six-season calendar corresponds to measured climate using 30 years of daily and monthly records (1985–2014) from 33 BMD weather stations.

Using an exhaustive contiguous search among circular calendar partitions across six climate variables (10,865 station-months), we identified a four-season partition (Winter, Pre-Monsoon, Monsoon, Post-Monsoon) as the preferred description of the monthly climatology. The traditional seasons group into these blocks in pairs, and resampling 33 stations and 30 years showed high partition stability (mean ARI of 0.931 and 0.884).
